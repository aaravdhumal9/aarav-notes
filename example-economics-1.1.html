1.1Demand

Definition

Demand is the quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to buy at different prices during a given time period, ceteris paribus.

Definition

Law of demand: as the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls, and as the price falls, the quantity demanded rises, ceteris paribus.

Why the demand curve slopes downward

  • Income effect: when price falls, buyers can afford more, because their real income has gone up.
  • Substitution effect: when price falls, the good becomes cheaper compared with other goods, so buyers switch towards it.
  • Diminishing marginal utility: each extra unit gives less satisfaction, so buyers will only buy more if the price is lower.

Movement along the curve and shift of the curve

A change in the price of the good itself causes a movement along the demand curve (a change in quantity demanded). A change in any other factor shifts the whole curve (a change in demand).

Non price determinants of demand (what shifts the curve)

DeterminantEffect
Income (normal good)Income rises, demand rises (shift right)
Income (inferior good)Income rises, demand falls (shift left)
Price of substitutesSubstitute gets dearer, demand for this good rises
Price of complementsComplement gets dearer, demand for this good falls
Tastes and preferencesGood becomes more fashionable, demand rises
Population and demographicsMore buyers, demand rises
ExpectationsBuyers expect higher future prices, demand rises now
Worked example

Demand is given by Qd = 100 − 2P. Find the quantity demanded when P = 20.

Step 1: put the price into the equation: Qd = 100 − 2(20).
Step 2: work it out: Qd = 100 − 40 = 60.
So at a price of 20, the quantity demanded is 60 units. A fall in price to 10 gives Qd = 80, which shows the law of demand.

Exam tip

Do not say "demand falls" when only the price of the good changed. That is a fall in quantity demanded (a movement along the curve). Only a non price factor shifts the curve. Marks are lost on this every year.

Not in textbook but there in IBDP syllabus

(Sample of how an added syllabus point would look.) Giffen goods and Veblen goods are exceptions where a higher price can lead to higher quantity demanded.

Key terms
  • Demand: willingness and ability to buy at different prices in a given period.
  • Quantity demanded: the amount bought at one specific price.
  • Ceteris paribus: all other factors held constant.
  • Normal good: demand rises when income rises.
  • Inferior good: demand falls when income rises.
Command terms to expect
  • Define: give the precise meaning, using the keywords.
  • Explain: give reasons, step by step, using a diagram.
  • Distinguish: make clear the differences (for example movement versus shift).

Quick check

1. What is the difference between a change in demand and a change in quantity demanded?

A change in quantity demanded comes from a change in the good's own price (movement along the curve). A change in demand comes from a non price factor (the whole curve shifts).

2. A rise in the price of a substitute does what to demand for this good?

It increases demand (shift right), because buyers switch to this good.

3. Name the three reasons the demand curve slopes downward.

Income effect, substitution effect and diminishing marginal utility.

1.2Supply

Definition

Supply is the quantity of a good or service that producers are willing and able to sell at different prices during a given time period, ceteris paribus.
